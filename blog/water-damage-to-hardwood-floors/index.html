Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Decatur, IL

Home / Blog / Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Decatur, IL

Few parts of a house are worth as much as its hardwood flooring, and few are as easily harmed by water. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. For Decatur, IL homeowners with hardwood floors, it is important to understand how to respond to water exposure and when professional restoration can save your floors versus when replacement is necessary.

How Water Damages Hardwood

Because wood is hygroscopic, it takes in moisture and gives it back as the conditions around it change. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. This causes cupping (edges rising higher than the center of boards), which is the first visible sign of moisture imbalance. If moisture exposure continues, boards may crown (center rising higher than edges as the top dries faster than the bottom) or buckle (lifting entirely off the subfloor). Mold is another risk of long exposure, since it grows unseen between the subfloor and the hardwood and may not be found until the floor comes up.

Can Water-Damaged Hardwood Be Saved?

Five things decide if the floor is salvageable: the speed of the professional drying response, how much water there was, the time the boards spent wet, what kind of hardwood and finish you have, and what shape the subfloor is in. If the water was clean and a professional begins drying inside of 24 to 48 hours, the hardwood is salvageable in many cases. Success depends on drying the wood gradually and under control, which lets it give up moisture evenly instead of cracking and splitting.

How Professionals Dry Hardwood Floors

Wood floors need a specialized approach that differs from the standard methods used on the rest of a structure. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Another option is to run a floor drying system under the subfloor, reaching it through the crawl space. The key is gradual, even drying, because drying too rapidly makes the surface dry faster than the core and leads to permanent cupping, cracking, and splitting.

When Replacement Is Necessary

Replacement is usually the answer if the subfloor is compromised or has mold on it, the boards have buckled or delaminated extensively, sewage, floodwater, or other contaminated water touched the wood, or the floor stayed wet longer than 48 to 72 hours.
